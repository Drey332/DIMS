You want breadth, reliability, and diversity of industrial contexts:

Category	Source	Why It Matters
Regulatory accident reports	- US CSB (Chemical Safety Board) – refinery & chemical fires   - MSHA (mining)  - BSEE (offshore)  - PHMSA (pipeline, hydrocarbon fires)  - HSE RIDDOR (UK)	Root-cause + barrier failure narratives, official findings

Public datasets	- Kaggle “Industrial Fire Dataset,” “Fire Department Incident Data”  - NFPA Fire Incident Reporting (NFIRS)	Quantitative frequency data by ignition source/material
Academic / standards	- NFPA, OSHA, IEC 60079, ISO 45001 fire safety sections	Baseline risk factors, compliance parameters

Build a single Fire Intelligence Lake that unifies them under one schema.

PHASE 2 — Structure & Enrich the Data (the “Bloomberg Terminal” of Fire)

1. Standardize with a Fire Taxonomy

Create a Fire Event Ontology with key attributes:

Dimension	Example Fields
Who/Where	Operator, site, location, asset type, operation type (FPSO, refinery, plant)
When	Date, time, shift, season, local conditions
What (event)	Ignition source (welding, electrical fault, friction, spontaneous), fuel type, containment status
Why (root cause)	Human error, equipment failure, maintenance lapse, static discharge, poor ventilation
Environment	Temperature, humidity, wind, confined/open space, sea state
Detection/Response	Sensor triggered?, detection delay, suppression method, response time
Outcome	Damage severity, downtime, fatalities, financial loss
Barriers	Which barriers failed (training, inspection, suppression, design)
Lessons	Text summary, recommended improvements

2. Enrich Each Event

Add live contextual enrichment for model training:
	•	Weather and metocean at time of incident
	•	Asset age, production phase, maintenance logs (if available)
	•	Similar past incidents (semantic clustering)

⸻

PHASE 3 — Train the Fire Intelligence Model

1. ML Stack

Goal: Predict probability & severity of fire given operating conditions + precursors.

Model Type	Use
NLP (Transformers, e.g., BERT)	Parse unstructured narratives from CSB, IMCA, MSHA reports into structured causal graphs.
Gradient-boosted trees (XGBoost/LightGBM)	Predict likelihood of fire from structured variables (environment, work type, inspections).
Time-series (LSTM/TFT)	Predict near-term fire probability given live sensor readings and metocean changes.
Graph Neural Networks	Learn interactions among systems (fuel, ventilation, electrical, human).
Causal Inference Models	Quantify which control failures most strongly cause ignition events.

Each new accident anywhere in the world becomes a new training datapoint.

2. Outputs
	•	Global Fire Risk Index (FRI): 0–100 score by activity & environment.
	•	Ignition Pathway Predictor: top 3 most likely causes given current work setup.
	•	Barrier Effectiveness Estimator: quantifies which barriers most reduce local risk.
	•	“Lessons in Context”: auto-summarized similar cases and outcomes (using NLP).

3. Explainability

Borrow Aladdin’s philosophy: every risk score is explainable.
Use SHAP or LIME to show why the model flags high risk — e.g.:

“This hot-work task has 72% similarity to Chevron El Segundo 2019 fire (welding + vapor + wind gust 28 kt + failed LEL monitor).”
